1 · Energy and power of a travelling wave

Wave energyCame back

Energy and power questions look like formula recall, but the marks go on signs and factors. The energy of a string element is biggest at the mean position and zero at the extremes. Once you use that, every sign question becomes: is the element moving toward the mean or away from it?

Worked example · the exact problem you saw (CTA16 Q19)

A thin element of a string carries a plane progressive sinusoidal wave moving along +x. E is the energy of the element and P is the rate of change of that energy. (A) P is positive if the slope and the displacement are both positive. (B) P is negative if the slope and the displacement are both negative. (C) P is negative if the velocity and the displacement are both negative. (D) P is positive if the velocity and the displacement are both positive.

Your answer was B and C. Correct answer: A and C. The portal does not print the key for multi-correct questions. A and C are my derivation, and your result (wrong, with B marked) agrees with it.

Step 1 · Start from where the energy is biggestAn element's energy is largest at the mean position (it moves fastest and the string there is steepest) and zero at the crest or trough. So E rises while the element moves toward the mean and falls while it moves away.
Step 2 · Turn the velocity options into toward or awayToward the mean means y and vy have opposite signs. Away means the same sign. Option C: v < 0 and y < 0, same sign, so the element moves away and P is negative. C is true. Option D: v > 0 and y > 0 is also away, so P is negative and D is false.
Step 3 · Turn the slope options into velocity optionsFor a wave moving along +x, vy = −v·slope. So a positive slope means the element moves down, and a negative slope means it moves up.
Step 4 · Check A and BOption A: slope > 0 gives vy < 0, and y > 0. Opposite signs, so toward the mean and P > 0. A is true. Option B: slope < 0 gives vy > 0, and y < 0. Opposite signs again, so P > 0, and 'P is negative' is false.
Step 5 · Confirm with the formulaTake y = A sinφ, φ = kx − ωt. Then E ∝ cos²φ and P ∝ sin2φ ∝ y × slope. So P is positive when y and slope have the same sign. That makes A true. B says P is negative when both are negative, so B is false.
Answer: A and C
The trap: marking B because 'negative and negative' sounds like a negative result. Always convert the option to toward or away from the mean first.

The shortcuts
  • Sign of P: an element's energy peaks at the mean position and is zero at the extremes. P is positive when the element moves toward the mean (y and vy have opposite signs) and negative when it moves away (same signs).
  • Slope to velocity: for a wave moving along +x, vy = −v·slope. For a wave along −x, vy = +v·slope. Use it only to turn a slope option into a velocity option.
  • Formulas: energy per length = ½μω²A². Average power = ½μω²A²v, with v = √(T/μ). In a travelling wave every element has KE = PE at every instant.
  • Energy per length of a string: KE = ½μ(∂y/∂t)² and PE = ½T(∂y/∂x)², with Tk² = μω². In a standing wave the energy swaps between KE and PE. At the instant every element passes through y = 0 it is all KE.
  • Rate of change, wave along either way: for y = A sinφ the energy per length is μω²A²cos²φ. If φ = kx − ωt (wave along +x) it changes at +μω³A² sin2φ. If φ = kx + ωt (wave along −x) the sign is minus. Read the direction from the sign between kx and ωt before anything else.
  • Nearest-integer answers: keep π = 3.1416. Using π² ≈ 10 turned 49.35 into 50 on your CTA16 Q27.
  • Two waves of amplitudes A₁ and A₂ travelling opposite ways: energy between two nodes = ½ρsω²(A₁²+A₂²)(π/k). Net power through a node = ½ρsω²(ω/k)(A₁²−A₂²).
Your CTA16 Q27, solved (key 49): μ = T/v² = 100/100² = 0.01 kg/m and ω = 2π·100 = 628.3 rad/s. P = ½μω²A²v = ½(0.01)(628.3)²(0.5×10⁻³)²(100) = 0.04935 W = 49.35 mW, so 49. With π² ≈ 10 the same steps give exactly 50 mW, which is your answer.
Your CTA17 Q23, solved (my derivation, the portal did not show the key): the waves are 8 sin(ωt−kx) and 6 sin(ωt+kx). Energy between two nodes = ½ρsω²(64+36)(π/k) = 50πρsω²/k. Option A says 25π, so A is incorrect. Net power through a node = ½ρsω²(ω/k)(64−36) = 14ρsω³/k. Option B says 25, so B is incorrect too. Options C and D depend on a figure that the portal text does not show.

2 · Interference: turn path difference into a phase

InterferenceFirst seen in CTA18

Interference questions are short once you set them up the same way every time: distances, then path difference, then phase, then add arrows. The two mistakes that cost you marks are adding amplitudes as plain numbers, and assuming every source is in phase. Skipping the question costs the same marks.

Worked example · the exact problem you saw (CTA18 Q36)

Three coherent sonic sources of wavelength λ sit on the x-axis at (−λ√11/6, 0), (0, 0) and (λ√11/6, 0). The intensity at the point (0, 5λ/6) due to each source alone is I₀. What is the resultant intensity? (A) 6I₀ (B) 7I₀ (C) 9I₀ (D) 5I₀

Your answer was C (9I₀). Correct answer: B (7I₀). The portal shows your answer as wrong and does not print the key. 7I₀ is my derivation, and it is one of the four options.

Step 1 · Find the three distancesMiddle source: 5λ/6. Each outer source: √((λ√11/6)² + (5λ/6)²) = λ√(11/36 + 25/36) = λ.
Step 2 · Path difference, then phaseOuter minus middle = λ − 5λ/6 = λ/6. Phase δ = 360° × (λ/6)/λ = 60°. The two outer sources have the same δ by symmetry.
Step 3 · Draw the three waves as equal arrowsEach wave has amplitude a, and I₀ ∝ a². Take the middle arrow along x. Each outer arrow is turned 60° from it. Place them head to tail (figure above).
Step 4 · Add the componentsx: a + 2a cos60° = 2a. y: 2a sin60° = √3 a. Resultant² = 4a² + 3a² = 7a².
Step 5 · Convert to intensity and check the shortcutI = 7 I₀. The general result for this layout is I = I₀(5 + 4cosδ), and δ = 60° gives 5 + 2 = 7.
Answer: B, 7I₀
Why 9I₀ fails: it needs all three in phase (3a). Dropping the y-part leaves 2a, which gives 4I₀. That is not even an option, and a value missing from the list is the clue that a part was dropped. The outer sources are 60° behind the middle one, so both parts count.

The shortcuts
  • Every time: distances, then path difference Δ, then phase δ = 360° × Δ/λ, then add equal-length arrows and square the length. I = |sum|².
  • Two equal sources: I = 2I₀(1 + cosδ) = 4I₀cos²(δ/2). Maximum at Δ = nλ, minimum at Δ = (n + ½)λ.
  • Middle source plus two symmetric outer ones: I = I₀(5 + 4cosδ), where δ is the phase of an outer source relative to the middle.
  • Walking along a line toward a source: Δ shrinks steadily to 0 far away. The farthest minimum is the first one where Δ = λ/2. The next one nearer has Δ = 3λ/2.
  • A source with its own lag: if a source lags the others by ε, add ε to the lag of its wave at the point. Total lag of each wave = path lag + source lag. Subtract the total lags to get the relative phase, then draw the arrows.
Your CTA18 Q23, solved (key 3): along the line through D₁ the path difference is Δ(y) = √(y² + 74.25) − y. At P, y = 6: √110.25 − 6 = 10.5 − 6 = 4.5. Q is the last minimum beyond P, so Q has Δ = λ/2 and P is the next one in, with Δ = 3λ/2. So 3λ/2 = 4.5 and λ = 3 m. Check: Q is where Δ = 1.5, at y = 24 m, and the next minima inward are at y = 6 and y = 1.2.

3 · Junctions and boundaries: two conditions do everything

JunctionsCame back

Wherever a string changes (two strings joined, a fixed end, a mass on the end) the same two conditions hold: the displacement is continuous, and the tension times the slope is continuous. Reflection rules, node conditions and boundary-mass problems all come from those two. Questions on this skipped twice in CTA17 and went wrong once in CTA18.

Worked example · the exact problem you saw (CTA17 Q36)

Two uniform strings are joined at J and stretched with tension T. String A has mass per length μ and length L. String B has 4μ and length 3L/2. Only normal modes with J as a node are excited, called the fundamental, 1st overtone, 2nd overtone, … in increasing frequency. Let E₀ = π²Ta²/L. If the amplitude at the antinodes of B is a in every mode, match the total energy of the composite string for: I) 1st overtone, II) fundamental, III) 3rd overtone, IV) 2nd overtone. List-II: P) 5/2, Q) 45/8, R) 40, S) 5/8, T) 10, U) 45/2. (A) I→P, II→S, III→T, IV→Q (B) I→T, II→P, III→R, IV→U (C) I→T, II→S, III→R, IV→Q (D) I→U, II→P, III→R, IV→T

Your answer was skipped. Correct answer: B. The portal does not print the key for this one. B is my derivation, and the energies I get (5/2, 10, 45/2, 40) are four of the six List-II values.

Step 1 · Speeds and mode frequenciesvA = √(T/μ) and vB = √(T/4μ) = vA/2. Each piece has nodes at both ends. A: f = n·vA/(2L). B (length 3L/2): f = m·vB/(2·3L/2) = m·vA/(6L).
Step 2 · Keep only the modes that fit both piecesEqual f needs n/(2L) = m/(6L), so m = 3n. The allowed modes are f = n·vA/(2L) with n = 1, 2, 3, 4. So the fundamental is n = 1, the 1st overtone n = 2, the 2nd overtone n = 3, the 3rd overtone n = 4.
Step 3 · Use the junction condition to link the amplitudesJ is a node, and T × slope must match on both sides. T is the same, so the slope amplitudes match: AAkA = a·kB. Since k = ω/v and vB = vA/2, kB = 2kA, so AA = 2a.
Step 4 · Energy of each pieceA standing wave with antinode amplitude A on length ℓ has energy ¼μω²A²ℓ. Here ω = 2πf = πn·vA/L, so μω²L = n²π²T/L. EA = ¼μω²(2a)²L = n²E₀. EB = ¼(4μ)ω²a²(3L/2) = (3/2)n²E₀.
Step 5 · Total, then matchE = (5/2)n²E₀. n = 1: 5/2 (P). n = 2: 10 (T). n = 3: 45/2 (U). n = 4: 40 (R). So 1st overtone → T, fundamental → P, 3rd overtone → R, 2nd overtone → U. That is I→T, II→P, III→R, IV→U.
Answer: B
The trap: a long stem with two strings. It breaks into four short steps: speeds, allowed modes, amplitude link at J, energy. Do step 1 and see where it leads.

The shortcuts
  • At any junction: displacement is continuous, and T × slope is continuous. With equal tensions the slope is continuous.
  • Pulse reaching a junction (impedance Z = √(Tμ), going from 1 to 2): r = (Z₁ − Z₂)/(Z₁ + Z₂) and t = 2Z₁/(Z₁ + Z₂). Light to heavy: reflected pulse inverted, transmitted pulse erect and shorter (the speed drops, the frequency stays). Heavy to light: reflected pulse erect. Energy share goes as Z·amplitude².
  • Composite string with J a node: write the allowed f for each piece, then keep the common ones. The amplitude link comes from equal slope amplitudes, A₁k₁ = A₂k₂.
  • Standing wave energy: ¼μω²A²ℓ for antinode amplitude A over length ℓ (a whole number of half-wavelengths).
  • Mass or spring at the end: write Newton's law for the end object, mψtt = −kψ − Tψx at the end, then put in the incident and reflected waves.
  • Pulse bouncing off a fixed end: it comes back the same size and inverted. Going from heavy (Z₁) to light (Z₂), t = 2Z₁/(Z₁+Z₂) can be above 1: the pulse is taller but carries less energy. Check with Z·amplitude²: the shares must add to 1.
  • Mass and spring at the end, string on the left: take incident cos(ωt − kx) and reflected cos(ωt + kx + φ). The end equation gives |reflected| = |incident| and tan(φ/2) = S/(kT), where S = kspring − mω² and kT = ωZ. Check: S → ∞ (a wall) gives φ = 180°, and S = 0 gives φ = 0.
Your CTA17 Q25, solved (skipped; my derivation, the portal did not show the key): the mass sits at x = 0 with the string on the x < 0 side. mψtt = −2k₀ψ − Tψx. Put in the incident and reflected waves, with S = 2k₀ − mω² and z = eiφ: S(A + Bz) = ikT(A − Bz). The two sides only match in size if B = A, which therefore always holds. φ = 0 needs S = 0, so m = 2k₀/ω² (option A). For m = 0, tanφ = 4Tkk₀/(T²k² − 4k₀²) (option D). Correct: A and D.
Your CTA18 Q33, rule check: string A is light (μ = M/4L) and B is four times denser, so Z doubles. The pulse goes light to heavy: r = (1 − 2)/(1 + 2) = −1/3 (inverted, smaller) and t = 2/3 (erect, and shorter because the speed halves). Pick the diagram that shows all three features.

1Single-correctStanding wave energyL4

A string of length L is fixed at both ends and vibrates as y = 2A sin(2πx/L) cos ωt (second harmonic). At the instant every element passes through y = 0, about what percentage of the string's total energy lies in the segment 0 ≤ x ≤ L/8? Take the exact value of π.

Pick one option.

Answer: C · 4.5 %
At that instant cos ωt = 0, so the slope is zero everywhere and PE = 0. All the energy is KE, with density ∝ sin²(2πx/L). Fraction = ℓ/L − sin(2kℓ)/(2kL) with ℓ = L/8 and k = 2π/L: 1/8 − sin(π/2)/(4π) = 0.125 − 0.0796 = 0.0454.
  • A: assumed the energy is spread evenly (1/8)
  • B: added the sine term instead of subtracting it
  • D: used the fundamental's k = π/L instead of 2π/L
