1 · Rotation: rolling, friction torque and "just after"

RotationCame back

Every rotation miss comes from one habit: a number was written before the two equations were. A rigid body always needs F = ma for its centre and τ = Iα about the centre (or a pivot), plus one link between them. Rolling, a pin, a collision and a friction patch are four different links.

CTA19 Q20 (impact on a rigid triangle of three particles): skipped, key 6.

Different errors, one gap. The older misses were wrong numbers inside the coupling: a friction-torque factor, which momentum is conserved, the spin energy. The newest are 'stopped at the static answer' (Q21) and a skip (Q20). The gap is the same each time: the two equations and the link were not written down first.

The shortcut
  • Write both equations first: F = ma for the centre, τ = Iα about the centre or a fixed pivot. Count the unknowns, then find the link.
  • Rolling without slipping is the link. On a surface with acceleration a_s: bottom-point acceleration = f/m + f r²/I = a_s. Solid cylinder 3f/m, solid sphere 7f/(2m), hoop 2f/m.
  • A flat disc turning on another, with uniform pressure, has friction torque (2/3) μ N R, not μ N R. Add up rings of friction over the area.
  • 'Just after release': the speed is zero but α is not. Use τ = Iα about the pin, then a_cm = α × r_cm, then F = ma_cm for the reaction. The static share is the wrong number.
  • Impact: with no outside impulse about a point, angular momentum about that point is conserved. Use the centre of mass for a free body and the hinge for a hinged one. Linear momentum is conserved only for a free body.
Worked example · the question you got wrong (CTA16 Q32)

A solid cylinder lies on a rough horizontal platform (coefficient of static friction μ) that moves in SHM of amplitude A along a line at right angles to the cylinder's axis. The cylinder rolls without slipping. The shortest period of the platform for which it still does not slip is 2π√(A/(xμg)). Find x.

Your answer was 4. Correct answer: 3.

The wording is how the portal recorded it. The key of 3 matches this reading.

Step 1 · Platform accelerationThe platform moves in SHM with amplitude A, so its largest acceleration is a_p = ω²A. The cylinder must keep up with it without slipping.
Step 2 · Centre of the cylinderFriction f from the platform pushes the cylinder forward: a_c = f/m.
Step 3 · SpinTorque about the centre: f R = (½ m R²) α, so α = 2f/(mR). This spin makes the bottom point move forward faster than the centre.
Step 4 · The link: no slippingThe bottom point has acceleration a_c + αR = f/m + 2f/m = 3f/m and must equal a_p. So f = m a_p / 3.
Step 5 · The limitFriction can give at most μmg: m a_p/3 ≤ μ m g, so a_p ≤ 3μg. With a_p = ω²A: ω² ≤ 3μg/A, so T ≥ 2π√(A/(3μg)). So x = 3.
Answer: x = 3
The trap: using the block rule a_p ≤ μg (that gives x = 1). A rolling body has a spin, so friction only has to supply a third of m·a_p. I cannot see your working, so I cannot say how 4 came about. A sphere would give 3.5 and a hoop 2, so check which moment of inertia you used.

2 · Energy and momentum: whose energy, whose distance

EnergyCame back

Energy and momentum questions with two moving parts are bookkeeping. The marks go on which energy: only the energy in the centre-of-mass frame can be stored in a spring or lost to heat, and an agent that pulls does work through its own displacement. Write the ledger first: who pushes, how far each thing moves, where the energy goes.

Same family, and it is still there in CTA19. Q29 (CTA16) and Q32 (CTA18) are one mistake seen from two sides: the wrong energy went into the equation (all the kinetic energy instead of the centre-of-mass part; the block's gain instead of the work of the outside force). CTA19 Q23 is a spring and centre-of-mass problem in a longer stem, and it was skipped.

The shortcut
  • Split the kinetic energy: KE = ½MV_cm² + KE(COM frame). The first part can never go into a spring or heat. For two bodies the second part is ½ μ v_rel² with μ = m₁m₂/(m₁ + m₂).
  • Largest stretch or compression of a spring: ½ μ v_rel² = ½ k x², if nothing was lost before that.
  • W_ext = ΔKE + heat + ΔPE. A hand or an engine holding a speed does work = force × its own displacement, even when all of it goes to heat.
  • Heat from sliding friction is f × relative sliding distance, not the distance on the ground.
  • A chain landing on a table: force = (dm/dt)v + weight already landed. For a freely falling chain this is 3mgx/l when a length x has landed.
  • A sticking collision loses energy. Find the speed after it from momentum, then use the energy after it, never the energy before.
Worked example · the question you got wrong (CTA16 Q29)

Two blocks of 3 kg and 6 kg are joined by a light spring (k = 200 N/m) at its natural length on a smooth floor. They are given speeds of 1 m/s and 2 m/s in opposite directions along the spring. Find the maximum extension of the spring, in cm.

Your answer was 67. Correct answer: 30.

The wording is how the portal recorded it. The key of 30 matches this reading.

Step 1 · Which energy can the spring hold?Total KE is ½·3·1² + ½·6·2² = 13.5 J. Part of that is the motion of the centre of mass, and no spring force can remove it.
Step 2 · Centre of massTake the 3 kg block at −1 m/s and the 6 kg block at +2 m/s (moving apart). V_cm = (3(−1) + 6(2))/9 = +1 m/s, so KE_cm = ½·9·1² = 4.5 J.
Step 3 · Energy in the centre-of-mass frame13.5 − 4.5 = 9 J. The same number from the formula: μ = 3·6/9 = 2 kg, v_rel = 3 m/s, ½·2·3² = 9 J.
Step 4 · At the largest extensionBoth blocks move with V_cm, so all 9 J is in the spring: ½·200·x² = 9, x² = 0.09, x = 0.30 m = 30 cm.
Step 5 · The slip to avoidUsing all 13.5 J gives x² = 0.135, about 37 cm. Your 67 does not match that, so I cannot tell how it came about.
Answer: 30 cm
The trap: putting all the kinetic energy into the spring. If the blocks start toward each other, the spring compresses first by the same 30 cm and stretches by 30 cm later, so the answer is the same either way.

The shortcut
  • Multi-correct: treat each option as its own true or false question and write the one line that proves or kills it.
  • When you find a true option, ask what follows from it: the same formula at a special point, its integral or derivative, a limit.
  • A numeric option is checked by computing it, not by feel. 'All four true' does happen, so do not doubt a verified option because the set looks big.
  • Skips: write the first equation before you decide to skip. If you can write one, you are not stuck.
  • A long stem is usually 3 or 4 short facts joined together. Split it, and check a list-match row by row, not all at once.
  • Give each question a cap of about 90 seconds: two equations and a direction. Only then move on.

(A) The speed approaches (P/k)^(1/3). (B) When the speed is half of that terminal value v_t, the acceleration is 7P/(4Mv_t). (C) The acceleration decreases at every step as the speed rises. (D) The car reaches v_t after a finite distance.

Your answer was skipped. Correct answer: A, B and C (D is false).

Your record for this question: skipped, correct options A, B and C (from my derivation).

Step 1 · The first equation (30 seconds)Power P = Fv gives the engine force F = P/v. So M a = P/v − kv². Every option below comes from this line.
Step 2 · A: terminal speeda = 0 means P/v = kv², so v_t³ = P/k. True.
Step 3 · B: acceleration at half the terminal speedAt v = v_t/2: P/v = 2P/v_t and kv² = kv_t²/4 = P/(4v_t) (using kv_t³ = P). So Ma = (2 − ¼)P/v_t = 7P/(4v_t). True.

1Single-correctFriction torqueL4

A uniform disc of mass m and radius R spins about its vertical axle at ω₀ and is lowered flat onto a second coaxial disc of mass 2m and radius R. The second disc is at rest and turns freely on a frictionless bearing. The coefficient of kinetic friction between the discs is μ, and the pressure over the contact is uniform. The weight mg of the upper disc presses on the lower one. Find the time until both discs turn at the same rate.

Pick one option.

Answer: C · ω₀R/(2μg)
Friction torque with uniform pressure: τ = (2/3) μ m g R, equal and opposite on the two discs. Upper: α₁ = τ/(½mR²) = 4μg/(3R), slowing. Lower: α₂ = τ/(½·2m·R²) = 2μg/(3R), speeding up. They match when ω₀ − α₁t = α₂t, so t = ω₀/(α₁ + α₂) = ω₀R/(2μg). (Check: the common speed is ω₀/3, which angular momentum also gives.)
  • A: treated the lower disc as identical to the upper one: α₂ = α₁
  • B: used friction torque μmgR with no factor 2/3
  • D: assumed the upper disc comes to rest: t = ω₀/α₁

A uniform rod of mass m and length L lies horizontal and is hinged at a point L/4 from one end. It is released from rest. Consider the instant just after release. Select all correct statements.

Select all correct options, then press Check answer.

Answer: A, C and D
About the hinge: I = mL²/12 + m(L/4)² = (7/48) mL² and the torque is mg(L/4), so α = 12g/(7L) (A). The centre is L/4 from the hinge: a_cm = αL/4 = 3g/7, downward. mg − R = m a_cm gives R = 4mg/7, upward (D). The end on the short side is L/4 from the hinge, so its acceleration is αL/4 = 3g/7, and it goes up (C). (The far end, 3L/4 away, accelerates at 9g/7, faster than free fall.)
  • B: zero speed is not zero acceleration. The rod has an angular acceleration, so its centre accelerates and the hinge carries less than mg.

3Multi-correctRolling on a plankL4

A uniform solid sphere of mass m and radius r rests on a long horizontal plank. The plank is pulled from rest with constant acceleration a, and the sphere rolls without slipping. The coefficient of static friction is 0.40. Take g = 10 m/s². Select all correct statements.

Select all correct options, then press Check answer.

Answer: A and B
The bottom point must match the plank: f/m + f r²/I = f/m + (5/2)f/m = 7f/(2m) = a, so f = 2ma/7. Then f ≤ μmg gives a ≤ 7μg/2 = 14 m/s² (A).   • C: forgot that the plank itself accelerates; relative to it the centre goes back at 10 m/s²
  • D: no slipping ties the bottom point to the plank, not the centre

4Single-correctImpact on a rodL4

A uniform rod of mass 4m and length L lies on a smooth horizontal table. A lump of clay of mass m moves along the table at speed v, at right angles to the rod, hits one end, and sticks. Find the speed of the lump just after the collision.

Pick one option.

Answer: B · v/2
Nothing outside acts along the table, so momentum and angular momentum about the combined centre of mass are conserved. The centre of mass is (4m·L/2)/(5m) = 2L/5 from the struck end, and V_cm = mv/5m = v/5. I_cm = 4mL²/12 + 4m(L/10)² + m(2L/5)² = (8/15)mL². Angular momentum about the centre: mv(2L/5) = I_cm ω, so ω = 3v/(4L). The lump moves with the centre plus its spin: v/5 + ω(2L/5) = v/5 + 3v/10 = v/2.
  • A: spin part only; forgot the centre's own velocity v/5
  • C: momentum only, with no rotation: v/5
  • D: the far end of the rod, which moves backward at v/4

8Multi-correctStatics with frictionL4

A uniform cylinder of mass m and radius r lies in a fixed V-groove with perpendicular faces inclined at 37° and 53° to the horizontal (take sin 37° = 0.6 and cos 37° = 0.8). Both faces are rough, with μ = 0.50. A slow, steady torque turns the cylinder about its axis. Select all correct statements.

Answer: A, B and C
With no torque (A): N₁ = mg cos37° = 0.8mg and N₂ = mg sin37° = 0.6mg. The faces are perpendicular, so the friction at one face lies along the other face's normal. 9Single-correctList-matchL4

A long board moves to the right at a steady 6 m/s on a smooth floor, held at that speed by an outside force. A piece of chalk is gently placed at rest on the board (μ = 0.30, g = 10 m/s²) and leaves a mark. The moment the chalk reaches the board's speed, the board is stopped at once and held at rest. The chalk then slides on until it stops.
List I: (I) distance the chalk moves on the floor before it reaches 6 m/s; (II) distance the board moves in that time; (III) length of the mark on the board; (IV) time from placing the chalk until it stops for good.
List II (SI units): (P) 4; (Q) 6; (R) 12; (S) 2.

Pick one option.

Answer: B · I→Q, II→R, III→Q, IV→P
The chalk's acceleration is μg = 3 m/s². It reaches 6 m/s after 2 s and moves ½·3·2² = 6 m (I→Q). The board moves 6 × 2 = 12 m (II→R). The mark is 12 − 6 = 6 m (III→Q). Then the chalk slides forward at 6 m/s, stopping after 6/3 = 2 s and 6²/(2·3) = 6 m, which retraces the same mark. Total time 2 + 2 = 4 s (IV→P).
  • A: took the chalk to move with the board in the first phase (I→R)
  • C: added the two slides, 6 + 6, but the second slide retraces the first (III→R)
  • D: stopped the clock when the chalk reached 6 m/s (IV→S)
